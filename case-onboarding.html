Case study

Getting new support agents ready faster

A remote, four-week onboarding program that took new T1/T2 agents from zero product knowledge to proficiency in four weeks, across 20+ cohorts and with attrition under 10%.

Client
Global music streaming platform
Team
T1/T2 support
My role
Trainer, learning designer and SME coach
Scale
20+ cohorts of 20+ agents, 4 weeks each

The situation

Every class brought together 20 or more new agents from several countries, all starting from zero. None of them knew the client's workflows, tools or tone of voice, and all of them had to be ready to handle real customers within four weeks.

The client set the overall agenda, with room for creativity in how it was delivered. Our team brought its own way of teaching to that agenda, and I contributed to shaping it.

Our training team is fully remote and well organized. Before each class starts, we hold a pre-training check call with every trainee to make sure they can access all the tools they need and know what the next four weeks will look like. Trainees arrive on day one ready to learn rather than troubleshooting logins.

My role

My work covered the whole cycle, from the materials to the classroom to the follow-up:

  • Running the training agenda for each class.
  • Delivering instructor-led training.
  • Developing learning materials built around specific learning goals.
  • Reviewing every trainee's cases and turning what I found into actionable feedback.
  • Tracking the key metrics (KPIs) for each cohort.
  • Coaching the subject-matter experts (SMEs) who support trainees.

What I designed

I designed two of the program's training modules, each built around clear learning goals. I also created several gamification tools to support learning, turning practice and review into games that keep a remote class engaged.

How I delivered it

Classes ran remotely, in one-hour sessions for groups of about 20. Each session combined instructor-led training with hands-on activities, so trainees could try things straight away and get immediate feedback.

After class, trainees moved into nesting: working real hands-on cases to practise, with SMEs on hand to help. I worked on the SME side to make that support as strong as the classroom. I helped SMEs build their coaching skills, reviewed their work through QA, and coached them directly so they delivered information the way we intended.

The aim for SMEs was to help trainees think for themselves: asking guiding questions and using visual aids instead of handing over the answer.

Results

  • 4 weekstime to proficiency, with attrition under 10%
  • 400+agents onboarded across 20+ cohorts
  • 100+classes led

What I'd do differently

I'd gamify more of the program, and make much more use of peer learning. With 20 people from different countries in every class, there's a lot of knowledge in the room, and trainees can learn as much from each other as from trainers and SMEs.
