Reply Runner

You're on the support desk at Harvest Box, a meal-kit delivery service. Customers write in, and the road splits into three possible replies. Run down the lane with the reply you'd send.

By the end you'll be able to

  • acknowledge how a customer feels before solving the problem
  • own an issue instead of deflecting it
  • write in clear, positive language without jargon
  • match your tone to the customer's
  • set expectations you can keep
  • say no kindly and offer what you can do

Three levels, fifteen customers. A wrong reply costs customer trust and a right one wins it back. Lose all three and the shift ends early.

Designer's notes

Reply Runner is a portfolio piece by Vincenzo Asaro. The company, customers and scenarios are invented, but they are modelled on the kind of situations new support advisors meet in their first weeks.

Objectives first, mechanics second

Every scenario is tagged with one of six skills, and each skill is a learning objective shown before play. The runner only does one job: turn "which reply would you send?" into a quick, repeatable decision.

Plausible wrong answers

Wrong lanes are mistakes real advisors make: deflecting to a courier, a scripted "sorry for any inconvenience", over-apologising, promising what can't be guaranteed. Learners practise telling good from almost-good, not good from absurd.

Stakes that forgive

Customer trust gives the run some tension, but every right answer wins a point back and each level starts fresh. Mistakes cost something without locking a learner out of the later content.

Feedback that teaches

A miss stops the run and explains why the reply falls short, shows a stronger one and why it works. A hit gets a one-line reason, so a lucky guess still teaches something.

A difficulty curve

Level 1 contrasts warm and cold replies. Level 2 brings subtler traps and tone matching. Level 3 adds pressure, boundaries and a safety-critical message, with less time to read.

Learner control

Relaxed pace removes the timer for slower readers or second-language speakers. Sending early earns a bonus, so confident learners can move fast without forcing everyone to.

Debrief and retrieval practice

The results screen breaks performance down by skill and gives a tip for the weakest ones. "Practise the ones I missed" replays only those scenarios, with the replies in a new order.
