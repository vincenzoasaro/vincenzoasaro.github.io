How I work

Whatever the subject, I design training around the real job: the tasks people actually do, the tools they use and the pressure they work under.

I learned that on the support floor, before I trained anyone, and it applies to any team. Most projects follow the same five steps. The order matters, because each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Find out what's actually wrong

    I talk to the people doing the work, their managers and anyone who reviews quality, and I look at the data. Sometimes the problem isn't a training problem at all, and it's better to say so early.

  2. Decide what people should be able to do

    I write objectives as things people can do on the job, not topics to cover. That keeps sessions focused and makes them measurable.

  3. Design for practice

    Short input, then lots of doing: simulated tasks, role-plays, case discussions. People remember what they practised, not what they were shown.

  4. Deliver and adjust

    I run sessions live, read the room, and change the pace when needed. Facilitator guides let other trainers deliver the same session consistently.

  5. Measure and improve

    Knowledge checks show what was learned. Performance data shows what changed on the job. Both feed the next version.
